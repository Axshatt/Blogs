If you've spent any time on X or Instagram lately, you've probably seen creators dropping slash-style prompts like `/reveal`, `/teardown`, and `/exploadedview` and turning ordinary product shots into cinematic mini-films with Gemini's video generation (Veo). The results look expensive. The prompts behind them are not complicated — they just follow a pattern most people never bother to learn.

So I broke that pattern down and built out a complete library: **50 ready-to-use video prompt commands**, organized into six categories, each with a plain-English explanation and a copy-paste template. Replace `[SUBJECT]` with your product, object, or a reference photo/clip, and you're generating.

Here's the full list.

---

## Why "commands" instead of just prompts?

A slash command isn't a special API feature — it's a mental shortcut. Instead of staring at a blank prompt box wondering how to describe what you want, you pick a command, swap in your subject, and go. Once you've used a few of these, you start noticing the shared skeleton behind every good video prompt:

**[Subject] + [What happens to it] + [Camera movement] + [Lighting/mood] + [Duration]**

That's it. Every command below is really just that formula, pre-filled for a specific visual effect.

---

## 🔧 1. Product Breakdown & Technical

These make objects feel engineered, precise, and premium — perfect for tech products, gadgets, and anything you want to look "designed."

1. **`/reveal`** — Camera pulls back and light rises as the subject is dramatically unveiled.
   > "An 8-second video: [SUBJECT] emerges from darkness as a single spotlight slowly rises, camera slowly pushes in, cinematic reveal, dust particles catching light, dramatic orchestral swell feel"

2. **`/teardown`** — Parts lift off one by one and settle into a neat layout.
   > "An 8-second video: [SUBJECT] disassembles itself piece by piece, each part lifting off and gliding into a neat flat-lay arrangement, smooth top-down camera, soft click sound cues, clean white surface"

3. **`/exploadedview`** — Parts separate outward along an axis, then reassemble.
   > "An 8-second video: [SUBJECT] explodes apart along a central axis, all components drifting outward in slow motion, camera slowly orbits, then parts reverse and snap back together, white studio background"

4. **`/crossection`** — Camera glides through a cutaway, revealing internals.
   > "An 8-second video: the camera glides directly through a cross-section cutaway of [SUBJECT], revealing internal layers and mechanisms in sequence, smooth continuous dolly move, clinical lighting"

5. **`/blueprint`** — Object draws itself in as animated blueprint linework.
   > "An 8-second video: [SUBJECT] is progressively drawn in as glowing white blueprint linework on a navy background, dimension lines animating in sequence, engineering-drawing style, steady camera"

6. **`/xray`** — A scan line sweeps across, revealing the x-ray interior.
   > "An 8-second video: a scanning line sweeps across [SUBJECT] revealing its x-ray interior structure as it passes, blue-white radiographic glow, slow linear camera pan, dark background"

7. **`/wireframe`** — The object materializes from a glowing wireframe mesh.
   > "An 8-second video: [SUBJECT] materializes from a glowing wireframe mesh into a fully solid rendered object, particle build-up effect, slow camera orbit, black background"

8. **`/schematic`** — Circuit-style nodes and lines animate across the subject.
   > "An 8-second video: glowing schematic circuit lines trace across [SUBJECT], nodes lighting up in sequence like a powering-on animation, dark technical background, steady close-up camera"

9. **`/anatomy`** — Labeled layers peel back to reveal the structure beneath.
   > "An 8-second video: labeled anatomical layers of [SUBJECT] peel back one at a time to reveal the structure beneath, textbook-illustration style, slow methodical pacing, cream paper background"

10. **`/techspec`** — Camera pans across the product as spec callouts animate in.
    > "An 8-second video: camera slowly pans across [SUBJECT] on a clean studio background as minimal spec-sheet callout lines animate in one by one, premium tech-launch video style"

## How to actually use these

1. **Pick a command** that matches the feeling you want — technical, cinematic, playful, or nostalgic.
2. **Swap in `[SUBJECT]`** with your product, object, or a reference photo/clip attached in Gemini.
3. **Adjust the duration** — 8 seconds is a solid default, but drop to 4s for a punchy hook or stretch to 15s for a fuller story. Reels and Shorts tend to perform best in the 6–10s range.
4. **Stack two commands** for a hybrid effect. `/exploadedview` + `/vintage` gives you an exploded diagram shot like it came out of a 1970s ad. `/hologram` + `/beforeafter` gives you a sci-fi transformation reveal.
5. **Always specify camera movement.** This is the single biggest lever separating a prompt that feels intentional from one that feels generic. Push-in, orbit, pan, static — pick one, every time.

## A few extra tips

- These are built for Gemini's video generation (Veo), and work best with a reference image or short clip attached rather than a blind text-only prompt.
- The commands aren't literal API syntax — you still need to type out the full template. Think of the slash as a memory aid, not a function call.
- Lighting and material modifiers (`marble`, `chrome`, `neon`, `golden hour`) can be dropped into almost any template to reskin the mood without changing the structure.
- Don't be afraid to under-describe. Gemini fills in a lot of visual detail on its own — the prompts above give it just enough direction without over-constraining the result.
