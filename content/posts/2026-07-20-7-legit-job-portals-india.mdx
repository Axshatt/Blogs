Every few months someone lands in my DMs with some version of: "I've been applying for 3 months and haven't heard back from anyone — is this normal?" And when I dig in, they've usually been applying on a sketchy aggregator site scraping expired listings, or responding to "offers" that arrived as unsolicited WhatsApp messages from a +91 number with no company email in sight.

The tech job market in India is genuinely competitive. It doesn't need the extra layer of fake postings, ghost listings, and outright scams that litter the bottom half of the job portal ecosystem. Before you burn three months submitting applications into a void, make sure you're playing on a platform where the jobs are real and the companies behind them are verifiable.

Most "best job sites" articles are thinly veiled affiliate pieces that list every platform alphabetically with zero opinion about any of them. This isn't that. I've used or know people who've gotten actual jobs through each of these seven platforms. I'll tell you what each is genuinely good for — and what it isn't.

## Before you start applying anywhere

A few things that apply across every platform on this list:

- **Resume in PDF format.** Not DOCX. PDFs render the same on every recruiter's screen and every ATS parser. DOCX doesn't.
- **GitHub or portfolio link on every profile.** For tech roles, "see my projects" still outweighs a second page of skill bullets.
- **Apply within 48 hours of a posting going live.** Most shortlisting happens in the first 48-72 hours. Being in the second wave often means being invisible.
- **Universal red flag:** Any posting asking for money upfront (registration fee, training fee, background check deposit), vague "work from home ₹50,000/week" language, or a recruiter who contacts you only through a personal WhatsApp or Gmail instead of a company domain. On any platform, these are scams. Every single one.

## 1. LinkedIn Jobs — Best for Knowing Exactly Who's Hiring

**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/

**What it does:** LinkedIn ties every job posting to a verified company page. Before you apply, you can see the actual company headcount, check if the hiring manager's profile is real, look for mutual connections who can give you context, and see if the company page has any activity at all. It's the one platform where the company has reputational skin in the game.

**Best for:** Mid-level to senior roles, any company type, and networking-first applications where you reach out to a recruiter before or after applying. The biggest unlock here isn't the job listing — it's the people layer around it.

**When to use it:** When you want to apply smart rather than at volume. A personalized connection request to the hiring manager with a one-line note explaining why you're a fit has a conversion rate that no amount of "Easy Apply" blasting will match.

**Pricing:** Free tier is enough for most. LinkedIn Premium (~₹2,600/month) gives InMail credits and "how you compare to other applicants" data — useful for a focused 2-week job sprint, not worth paying indefinitely.

**Make it yours:** Enable "Open to Work" but set it to **Recruiters only**, not the green banner. The green banner signals to your current employer that you're looking — and makes some hiring managers assume you're desperate rather than selective.

## 2. Naukri.com — The Volume Play for India

**URL:** https://www.naukri.com/

**What it does:** India's largest job portal, full stop. Naukri has the highest listing density of any platform for Indian roles — IT services, product companies, BFSI, e-commerce, startups, and everything in between. Its coverage across Tier 2 cities (Pune, Hyderabad, Chennai, Bengaluru beyond Whitefield) is genuinely better than any other platform.

**Best for:** Anyone in India looking for a full-time role across any experience level or industry. Also useful for salary benchmarking — the salary insights on listings are more calibrated to the Indian market than Indeed or LinkedIn.

**When to use it:** As your primary search platform if you're targeting India-based full-time roles. Cast a wide net here first, then follow up on promising leads through LinkedIn.

**Pricing:** Free. Naukri's paid "Resume Writing" and "Priority Applicant" upsells exist but the evidence that they move the needle is thin. Skip them.

**Make it yours:** Fill out your Naukri profile fully — their search algorithm heavily weights profile completeness. An 80% complete profile means your resume shows up in recruiter searches for your skillset. Also set keyword alerts for specific companies you're targeting; it surfaces new postings before they get flooded.

## 3. Internshala — The Right Tool for Freshers and Students

**URL:** https://internshala.com/

**What it does:** Internshala is built specifically for internships and entry-level roles in India. This matters because companies post here knowing the audience — you're not competing against someone with five years of experience for a role that was never meant for them. The platform is specifically for students (2nd-4th year) and recent graduates, and that's actually its strength.

**Best for:** Students, recent graduates, anyone looking for their first role or a legitimate part-time remote internship with an Indian company.

**When to use it:** While you're still in college or just graduated and don't have enough experience to compete for full-time listings on Naukri. Post-3 years experience, it's not the right platform anymore — the listings won't match what you're worth.

**Pricing:** Free. Their "Internshala Training" courses are paid but optional and entirely separate from the job listings.

**Make it yours:** Write a custom cover letter every single time. Internshala listings get high application volumes and a one-paragraph note explaining why you specifically want *this* internship at *this* company converts far better than a blank apply click.

## 4. Indeed — The Global Firehose With Salary Data

**URL:** https://www.indeed.com/

**What it does:** Indeed aggregates listings from company career pages, third-party job boards, and direct postings. The volume is massive — largest global job site by listing count. What separates it from just being a big scraper is that salary estimates and employer reviews appear right on the listing before you click through. You get market context without opening another tab.

**Best for:** Global job searches, remote-first roles, and getting a fast market snapshot of what a specific role pays across different companies. For India-specific listings, the coverage isn't as deep as Naukri — but for remote roles or companies with international hiring pipelines, Indeed is often where those postings live first.

**When to use it:** Alongside Naukri, not instead of it. Also useful when you're evaluating whether a salary offer is market rate for your role.

**Pricing:** Free. Indeed Sponsored listings bias search results toward paying companies, but date + salary + job type filtering still surfaces good organic listings.

**Make it yours:** Set email alerts for specific company names combined with your target role. Indeed's alert system catches new listings faster than most platforms — you can be in the first wave of applicants if your alert is properly configured.

## 5. Glassdoor — Due Diligence Before You Apply or Accept

**URL:** https://www.glassdoor.com/

**What it does:** Glassdoor isn't primarily a job board — it's a company intelligence layer that happens to have job listings. The core value is real employee reviews, verified salary ranges broken down by role and location, and interview experience reports from people who've actually gone through the process. Before you accept an offer from a company, this is where you find out if their culture actually matches what their recruiter told you.

**Best for:** Researching a company before applying or before signing an offer letter. If you have two competing offers and want to know which workplace won't burn you out in 6 months, Glassdoor is the only place to get that signal at scale.

**When to use it:** Every time you're seriously considering applying somewhere or evaluating an offer. Takes 5 minutes and has saved more than a few people from nightmare jobs with great-sounding JDs.

**Pricing:** Free with a Glassdoor account. You need to upload your resume or write a company review to unlock full review access — minor one-time friction worth clearing.

**Make it yours:** Look specifically at reviews from the last 6-12 months, not the overall rating. Companies change fast. A 2.8 overall rating from a bad management era 3 years ago is less meaningful than 10 recent 1-star reviews all mentioning the same manager's name. Recency matters more than averages.

## 6. Foundit (formerly Monster India) — Solid, Boring, Reliable

**URL:** https://www.foundit.in/

**What it does:** Foundit (rebranded from Monster India) is one of India's oldest job portals. That age means deep relationships with traditional corporates, large MNCs, and established Indian companies whose HR teams have been using this platform for years. It's not exciting. It works for what it is.

**Best for:** Full-time roles at established Indian companies, large corporates, and MNCs that have had direct HR integrations here for a long time. Tata group companies, Infosys, Wipro, HCL — these companies have had standing relationships with Monster/Foundit going back years.

**When to use it:** As a supplementary platform alongside Naukri if you're specifically targeting legacy corporate India and aren't finding the listings you want elsewhere. Don't make it your primary source — Naukri has more coverage.

**Pricing:** Free.

**Make it yours:** Keep your profile updated because corporate recruiters at traditional companies still search Foundit's database actively rather than waiting for applications. An outdated profile means you're invisible to their searches even if you're a perfect fit.

## 7. Wellfound (formerly AngelList Talent) — For Startups With Transparent Compensation

**URL:** https://wellfound.com/

**What it does:** Wellfound is built specifically for startup hiring and the one thing it does that no other platform on this list does: salary and equity ranges are shown upfront on every single listing. Not "competitive compensation." Not "as per industry standards." Actual numbers before you apply. You know whether the range makes sense for you before you waste anyone's time.

**Best for:** Anyone targeting early-to-mid-stage startups (seed to Series B/C), especially in SaaS, fintech, developer tools, or deeptech. Also useful if you want to understand a startup's funding stage and trajectory before applying — that context is baked into every company page.

**When to use it:** When you're specifically targeting the startup ecosystem rather than established companies. The listing volume is smaller than Naukri or Indeed, but the signal quality is higher — companies posting here are actively hiring, not maintaining ghost listings.

**Pricing:** Free for job seekers. No premium tier.

**Make it yours:** Fill out your salary and equity preferences honestly when setting up your profile. Wellfound uses these to match you with companies whose compensation ranges overlap with what you want. If you leave equity at ₹0 because you don't know how to value it, you'll filter out some legitimately good opportunities at well-funded startups.

## FAQ

### How many of these should I be active on at once?

Realistically three: LinkedIn (always), Naukri or Indeed as your primary volume platform, and either Internshala (fresher) or Wellfound (startup-focused). More than three and you'll spend more time managing profiles than actually applying and following up.

### I've been applying for months and getting zero responses — what's actually wrong?

Three most common issues in order of frequency: your resume isn't parsing correctly through ATS (tables, columns, and graphics cause this — use a single-column plain PDF), you're applying to roles where you're genuinely underqualified on paper, or you're applying too late and the shortlist is already done by the time your application lands. Set job alerts, apply within 48 hours, and test your resume through an ATS checker before you submit it anywhere.

### Is LinkedIn Premium worth paying for?

If you're doing an active job search sprint and will actually use InMail, yes for 1-2 months. If you're passively open to opportunities, no. Everything else on this list — Naukri, Indeed, Glassdoor, Wellfound — is free and that's genuinely fine.

### What about Unstop, Cutshort, or HackerEarth Jobs?

They exist and some people get hired through them. Unstop is worth checking for campus hackathons and competitions. Cutshort has a decent tech-specific listing pool. But for the core of your search, the seven above have better listing density and lower noise. Don't spread across ten platforms — you'll be half-present everywhere.

### How do I know if a posting is a scam even on a legit platform?

Four flags: (1) they ask for money at any stage — registration, training, equipment, background check deposit — that's a scam, (2) the job description is so vague it could describe literally anything ("dynamic individual, work from anywhere, unlimited income"), (3) the recruiter contacts you only through personal WhatsApp or Gmail rather than a company email domain, (4) they make an offer after a single WhatsApp call with no real interview. If you see any of these, close the tab.

### Should I apply to the same opening on multiple platforms?

If the same job appears on both Naukri and Indeed, it's the same opening at the same company. Applying twice doesn't help and some ATS systems flag duplicate applicants. One application per opening through whichever platform you find it on first.

### I'm targeting big tech in India (Google, Microsoft, Flipkart, Swiggy) — which platform?

LinkedIn, and specifically the referral path through LinkedIn. FAANG and large Indian tech companies have strong internal referral cultures. A referral from someone you've had a genuine interaction with converts significantly better than a cold application, no matter how well-optimized your resume is. Apply directly too, but prioritize finding a referral contact.

### How important is LinkedIn versus my resume document?

In 2026, LinkedIn *is* effectively the resume for tech roles. A fully built-out LinkedIn profile with recommendations, project details, and skill endorsements is what recruiters check before they open your PDF. Don't treat your LinkedIn as a backup profile you update once a year.

### What's the biggest mistake freshers make on these platforms?

Applying to 200 jobs in a spray-and-pray approach with a generic resume and no cover letters. 20 targeted applications with a tailored resume and a genuine cover letter will get you more interviews than 200 untailored ones. Quality over volume is counterintuitive when you're anxious, but it's true.

## What's next

If you're a student or fresher in India: start with Internshala for your first internship, build your GitHub in parallel, and treat LinkedIn as a long-term investment even before you're actively job hunting. The connections you make while you're still in college are worth more than the ones you scramble for when you're stressed and unemployed.

If you're mid-career and making a switch: LinkedIn plus Naukri is enough if you're doing it right. "Right" means a resume that clears ATS, applying within 48 hours of a listing going live, and sending a short connection note to the recruiter alongside your application. Most people skip at least one of these.
