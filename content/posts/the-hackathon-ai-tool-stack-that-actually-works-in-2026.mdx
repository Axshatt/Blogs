# The Hackathon AI Tool Stack That Actually Works in 2026
 
Every few weeks a "hackathon toolkit" graphic does the rounds — one tool per problem, clean and shareable. Most of them are copy-pasted from the last one. I went through this exact list tool by tool, checked what's still alive, what quietly shut down, and what the community actually recommends right now. Here's the stack, with the dead link fixed.
 
## Idea Research → [F5Bot](https://f5bot.com/)
 
The old go-to here was GummySearch — it isn't anymore. GummySearch stopped taking new signups in November 2025 after it couldn't secure a commercial license for Reddit's API, and the whole platform goes dark for good by the end of November 2026. If you're building a "hack" list right now and GummySearch is still on it, that's a dead link waiting to happen.
 
F5Bot is the closest free replacement: set up keyword alerts, and it emails you the moment someone on Reddit or Hacker News mentions a problem your idea could solve. No dashboard to babysit, no subscription.
 
## Trend Discovery → [Exploding Topics](https://explodingtopics.com/)
 
Still the right call. Built by Brian Dean (Backlinko) and Josh Howarth, it surfaces topics and products before they go mainstream instead of just showing you what's already peaked, which is what Google Trends does. The free tier is enough to browse; Pro is worth it if you're validating an idea for real, not just skimming for a demo.
 
## AI UI Design → [Magic Patterns](https://www.magicpatterns.com/)
 
A solid YC-backed pick for turning a prompt into a real UI fast, with export to Figma or React. If you want the more mainstream alternative in the same slot, [v0 by Vercel](https://v0.dev/) is worth having open in a second tab — bigger free tier, and it's started doing light backend scaffolding too.
 
## Full-Stack AI Builder → [Bolt.new](https://bolt.new/)
 
Prompt-to-deployed-app in one flow, no local setup. In head-to-head comparisons it tends to edge out the competition on speed and how closely it follows your prompt. [Lovable](https://lovable.dev/) is the one people mention as the friendlier alternative if you're less comfortable poking at code afterward.
 
## Backend → [Supabase](https://supabase.com/)
 
No real competition for this slot. Postgres, auth, storage, and realtime out of the box, with a free tier that comfortably covers a hackathon weekend. This is the one nobody in any dev thread argues with.
 
## AI Models → [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/)
 
One API key, access to hundreds of models — including free-tier ones — across every major provider. For a judged build where you don't want to burn hours on API key management, this is the correct default.
 
## Deploy → [Railway](https://railway.app/)
 
Here's the part most toolkit graphics leave out: Railway isn't free anymore in the way it used to be. Since 2023 it's a one-time $5 trial credit good for 30 days, then $5/month minimum. For an actual hackathon that's plenty — $5 easily covers a 24–48 hour build — so it's still the right pick for the event itself.
 
If you want the project to stay live *after* judging without paying, [Render](https://render.com/) is the better call — it has a genuine standing free tier (web service + Postgres + static frontend, no card required). The tradeoff is your app spins down after 15 minutes idle and takes about a minute to wake back up on the next visit, so it's fine for a portfolio piece, not for something you're actively demoing live.
 
## The takeaway
 
Six of these seven picks are still the right answer in 2026. The only real fix is swapping GummySearch for F5Bot — everything else just needed a footnote, not a replacement.
